The visible crisis

I've lived with NVIDIA GPUs for about 26 years. I still remember buying a GeForce card with 128MB of memory. Yes, MB. Not even GB.

For me, NVIDIA was the brand to trust to bring value into everyday life. That history is part of why I want to understand what's happened to the company.

A leadership crisis is easier to describe than a system failure. There is a recognisable person, a departure and a replacement. The harder work starts when we ask which decisions depended on that person, who could challenge them and what continues to depend on the same assumptions after they leave.

On 8 November 2026, Jensen Huang publicly admitted infidelity. NVIDIA announced an independent investigation into reporting that its founder and chief executive had been targeted by a foreign intelligence effort. The board said it then had no reason to believe US national security or company secrets had been compromised. That was its position at the start of an inquiry, rather than a published result. [PG03][PG04]

The SEC record is more precise than the shorthand of a Thursday resignation. The board accepted Huang's immediate resignation as president, CEO and director on 11 November. The announcement followed on 12 November. Colette Kress became interim CEO while remaining CFO; Harvey C. Jones became board chair. The distinction matters when we reconstruct what happened and when investors learned about it. [PG01][PG02]

The intelligence allegation needs equally careful language. Reuters attributed to The New York Times the account that US authorities had concluded Alice Huang, a businesswoman associated with NVIDIA customer Megaspeed, was a Chinese intelligence asset. The underlying assessment was not available to this investigation. China's foreign ministry rejected the allegations. No public adjudication of espionage was obtained in this research. [PG22][PG20][PG21]

Those limits leave a substantial public-interest story. It concerns a chip company, its customers, the governments controlling access to its products and an industry financing enormous amounts of infrastructure. My question is how those responsibilities and dependencies were distributed. Personal conduct brings attention to that question; it cannot supply the answer.

Years before the headlines

The export-control chronology begins well before the current disclosures. NVIDIA's August 2022 filing records US licensing requirements for its A100 and H100 products notified on 26 August and disclosed on 31 August. The broader October 2022 controls followed, with provisions taking effect on different dates. In October 2023, NVIDIA reported accelerated applicability of new controls to several of its data-centre products. [PG28][PG08][PG29][PG09]

Megaspeed also has a history before its AI identity. 7Road's exchange-filed 2022 annual report lists Singapore's 7Road International, incorporated on 28 September 2015, as an indirectly wholly owned subsidiary. The company's later transformation should not be mistaken for its incorporation. [CS07]

Reporting describes a March 2023 transfer to British Virgin Islands company Huang Le Limited and a November transfer to Swiftdata. Reuters reports Swiftdata as Megaspeed's sole owner. The original historical registry profiles were not independently obtained, so those transfers remain attributed reporting. A registered shareholder, a director and a person exercising influence are different categories. [CS08][CS05]

An ownership change can have an ordinary commercial explanation. It can also make due diligence harder if the people responsible for a purchase, the registered buyer and the ultimate beneficiary differ. Establishing concealment would require evidence of intent, inaccurate declarations or a misleading control structure. A sequence of transfers alone does not establish it.

The most useful chronology therefore has two clocks. One records the transaction, interview or government decision. The other records the source that makes it public. A later disclosure can change our understanding of an earlier event without proving that the event was caused by the circumstances disclosed later.

Follow the chips, carefully

A supply-chain diagram becomes misleading when every arrow means the same thing. A chip designer supplies a product. A server manufacturer integrates it. A distributor or infrastructure operator may buy the system. A financier may hold security over it. A customer may rent computing time. Those relationships can coexist around one machine, with different responsibilities attached to each.

Physical movement, ownership, remote access, re-export and the provision of controlled technology need separate evidence. A processor installed in Malaysia and used remotely by a customer elsewhere has not thereby been physically shipped to that customer's country. Equally, an offshore installation does not establish compliance with every end-user, ultimate-parent or end-use restriction. [PG14]

Singapore's official account dates its domestic server-declaration investigation to February 2025. It records a BIS post-shipment check and a police interview on 24 June 2025. MHA says servers transited Singapore to Malaysia and their subsequent destinations were being checked. That identifies a route and an unresolved question; it does not settle the final destination of every machine. [CS01]

The limits of the Singapore outcome are specific. MHA reports insufficient evidence of server-declaration offences against Alice Huang, a warning for inaccurate police information and a re-entry bar. She departed on 26 February 2026. Megaspeed and six individuals remained under investigation in November. Singapore says it did not investigate her for espionage. These are distinct findings and jurisdictions. [CS01]

Malaysia's MITI said on 13 November that it had never cleared Megaspeed or its affiliate Speedmatrix. Routine visits were not clearance. It described checks with customs over possible re-exports and distinguished local import rules from permit requirements for subsequent exports, re-exports, transshipment and transit. Neither an investigation nor an absence of a concluded violation should be turned into the opposite finding. [CS03]

Megaspeed's own 12 November response said it was cooperating with BIS and had retained a US law firm for a GPU census and compliance review. That is a relevant response. The announcement does not provide the resulting inventory or establish that the review has completed. [CS04]

A 13 November Bloomberg report added a reported Shanghai chip link. Its original body and underlying documents were inaccessible here; only limited metadata and its opening description were retrieved. That update belongs in the record, with the access limitation beside it. I cannot claim to have independently traced those chips. The defensible next step is transaction-level verification: serial numbers, invoices, licences, installation records and dated onward movements. [CS12]

Follow the decisions

The policy story contains changes of direction that can be documented without inventing a secret bargain. NVIDIA disclosed an H20 licence requirement notified on 9 April 2025. In July, its own account described Huang's meetings with Trump and policymakers and assurances that H20 licences would be granted. That documents advocacy and the company's account of an assurance about licensing. It does not establish that a particular meeting improperly caused a decision. [PG10][PG11]

There is also a direct lobbying record. NVIDIA's second-quarter 2026 disclosure reported $1.25 million in lobbying expenses and included semiconductor and export-control issues. Advocacy is part of the documented commercial strategy. Assessing whether officials acted properly requires the decision process, screening evidence and legal authority, rather than an inference drawn from access alone. [PG24]

The January 2026 H200 rule illustrates why headlines are an unreliable compliance system. Effective 15 January, it introduced conditional case-by-case review for certain US exports to China and Macau. It did not authorise every shipment or erase more restrictive treatment of re-exports and certain offshore Chinese-parent entities. BIS guidance in May separately stressed that existing headquarters and ultimate-parent licensing requirements continued despite non-enforcement of the newer AI Diffusion requirements. [PG13][PG12][PG14][PG27]

The product names matter, but they cannot replace that analysis. H100, H200 and H20 sit in a dated regulatory history; a Blackwell-generation label cannot establish a shipment's classification or licence status on its own. For any actual transaction, the investigator needs the precise product, technical specification, destination, recipient, controlling parent, end use and rules effective on that date. This research obtained no complete shipment-level licensing ledger. [PG28][PG10][PG13][PG12]

Congressional records show the oversight response. A 4 November House letter requested a closed-door briefing on 18 November. Warren and Coons requested preservation of NVIDIA-related records on 11 November. Wyden and Neal's 12 November letter questioned the administration's chip approvals and handling of the controversy. These documents establish what lawmakers asked and why; quoted newspaper allegations retain their original evidentiary limits. [PG17][PG18][PG19]

Reporting that Commerce altered its Megaspeed investigation after NVIDIA legal contact deserves scrutiny. The original New York Times investigation was inaccessible here. The available secondary account includes Commerce's defence of its procedure and NVIDIA's description of routine information-sharing. Establishing an improper intervention would require the actual instructions, case-transfer records and reasons. A sequence in which contact precedes a change is a lead for investigation, not proof of corruption. [PG23][PG18][PG19]

Follow the money, and label the arrows

The AI economy is where this story becomes larger than one executive. A supplier can help finance a customer, sell equipment into that customer's infrastructure and agree to buy some of its computing capacity. Each agreement may have a legitimate purpose. Together they can transmit the same demand shock through several balance sheets. [EM02][EM03]

CoreWeave makes those roles visible. Its September 2025 filing describes a NVIDIA order initially valued at $6.3 billion for qualifying residual unsold capacity, with conditions and termination rights. A January 2026 announcement records a separate $2 billion NVIDIA equity investment. Equipment supply, equity ownership and a conditional capacity purchase create different rights and obligations. Calling them all a loan would obscure the mechanism. [EM02][EM03]

NVIDIA's July-quarter filing says one direct customer accounted for 16% of quarterly revenue, without naming it. That measure concerns direct sales, rather than every organisation ultimately using the machines. It would be incorrect to identify that customer from a list of well-known cloud operators. [EM01]

The capital structure matters as much as the equipment. CoreWeave's September-quarter filing disclosed $17.6 billion of outstanding delayed-draw loans secured by underlying assets and pledged contracted cash flows. It identified Microsoft and OpenAI as significant customers and described concentration, power, supply and financing risks. This is evidence of dependencies acknowledged in a filing, rather than evidence that a customer has defaulted. [EM05]

Its November earnings release reported $3.661 billion of quarterly revenue and $207 million of GAAP operating income, alongside $846 million of net interest expense and a $709 million net loss. Growth and financing pressure can coexist. An infrastructure operator still has to pay for capital, electricity and equipment wear while turning contracted demand into collected cash. [EM04]

Depreciation adds another assumption to inspect. CoreWeave's annual filing uses a six-year estimated useful life for technology equipment. That is an accounting estimate. The economic questions are how long particular generations remain useful, what rental prices they earn as newer hardware arrives and what lenders can recover from collateral. A long useful life can be reasonable; its reasonableness depends on evidence about reuse and cash generation. [EM06]

NVIDIA's own July-quarter filing disclosed $99 billion of equity investments, $25 billion of equity-investment commitments and $36 billion of AI-cloud commitments. It also described an August arrangement for conditional credit support capped at $105 billion around an SB Energy/OpenAI campus lease. The guarantee phases were expected to begin with lease commencement from fiscal 2029 and depended on specified defaults. These amounts cannot be added into a total supposedly already spent, lent or currently payable. [EM01]

The direction of each flow changes the risk. Equity absorbs investment losses. A capacity purchase creates a service obligation with contractual conditions. Secured lending creates repayment and collateral claims. A credit guarantee creates a contingent payment obligation. Equipment revenue is another flow again. A useful map preserves those distinctions even when the same organisations appear in several roles.

There are also announcements whose scale is easy to misread. NVIDIA's August financing-platform memoranda targeted more than $500 billion of third-party capital over time, subject to final agreements. That was a mobilisation target, rather than a completed capital raise. OpenAI's February announcement identified $30 billion of NVIDIA participation; its March announcement described a closed round with $122 billion of committed capital overall. Announced intentions, commitments and disbursed money are different stages. [EM07][EM09][EM10]

Reciprocal commercial relationships can accelerate deployment, reduce coordination costs and give suppliers confidence to expand. That is the strongest ordinary commercial explanation. The resilience question remains: if an end customer's demand disappoints, which capacity commitments, equity values, collateral assumptions and guarantees are exposed together? Independent contracts do not necessarily create independent risks.

The decisive economic test is durable demand and cash generation from customers outside the financing loop. The records examined here establish overlapping roles and obligations. They do not provide a transaction-level audit proving that revenues are fictitious, that financing has been withdrawn after the resignation or that every customer depends on supplier support. The controversy makes the dependency map more urgent to inspect; it did not create that map.

The organisation behind the individual

Governance should be assessed against the responsibilities the organisation actually assigned. NVIDIA's May proxy described every director except Huang as independent under Nasdaq rules. It described a lead-director structure without a chair and written conflict and related-party policies. Its governance policies assign board oversight and provide for independent advice. Those documents establish the intended control system, rather than its performance in this case. [PG15][PG16]

The November response includes concrete institutional actions. The independent directors retained Joon Kim and Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz on 10 November. The leadership transition appointed an interim chief executive and a chair. It would be premature to treat those actions as proof that every previous control failed, or that the investigation has reached a conclusion. [PG05][PG01][PG02]

The unresolved question is how concerns reached people with authority to act. An organisation can have an independent board on paper while information, relationships and practical influence remain concentrated. Establishing that happened here requires records of notification, escalation, conflicts and decisions. A policy's existence cannot settle the question; neither can the fact that its subject was successful.

Personal misconduct becomes a corporate matter when it affects conflicts, supervision, information access, disclosure or the organisation's ability to make independent decisions. Those mechanisms deserve examination without treating private embarrassment as a substitute for material harm. The scope of an investigation should follow the responsibilities involved.

Kress retaining the CFO role while serving as interim CEO is also a practical allocation of responsibility. It provides continuity and concentrates duties during the transition. The next assessment should examine who can challenge major commitments, which decisions require additional approval and how the permanent succession process protects that challenge. [PG01][PG02]

Prices show a reaction, not an explanation

The completed 12 November session shows a measurable divergence. NVIDIA closed at $166.19, down 4.34% on the day. AMD rose 5.86%, Broadcom 5.54%, while the SOXX semiconductor ETF fell 0.26%. Those are closing comparisons. Reports of a much larger NVIDIA intraday fall describe a different measurement. [EM13][EM14][EM15][EM16][RS01]

Reuters' 11 November closing report also described CoreWeave's results, AI-spending sentiment, inflation and interest-rate concerns. Those are plausible concurrent influences. Investors can mark up a competitor because they expect an opportunity. Demonstrating that opportunity became revenue requires subsequent procurement decisions, signed contracts and delivered capacity. A price gain identifies a market beneficiary over a specified interval. It supplies no evidence that the beneficiary caused the crisis.

What the evidence does not tell us

Three explanations remain worth testing. Personal conduct may have triggered a governance crisis. An existing geopolitical dispute may have become a corporate crisis. Several issues may have developed separately before converging through the disclosures. The immediate company records support the first explanation; the earlier export-policy history supports the second's context. The third currently offers the broadest working description, while the degree of independence remains unresolved. [PG01][PG02][PG03][PG04][PG28]

None of those explanations establishes a coordinated intelligence operation linking every transaction and policy outcome. That stronger account would need authenticated communications, instruction, information transfers or comparable evidence. A graph of people and companies cannot provide intent merely by drawing enough connections.

The same discipline applies to apparently reassuring accounts. Singapore's correction of intelligence-related reporting does not resolve a US assessment. Its limited individual disposition does not clear every company transaction. Megaspeed's compliance-review announcement does not provide the review's results. NVIDIA's preliminary position does not substitute for its independent investigators' findings. [CS02][CS01][CS04][PG04]

There are identifiable ways to change this assessment. Certified ownership records could establish the control chain. A reconciled hardware ledger could resolve an inventory discrepancy. Licence files and enforcement instructions could clarify whether decisions followed the applicable process. A completed board report could establish who knew what and whether company information was compromised. Until then, the unanswered questions should stay visible.

The engineering problem

When I examine a complex system, I want to know what happens when a component becomes unavailable, behaves unexpectedly or carries an assumption nobody else can verify. The same habit is useful when looking at an organisation. It does not turn an engineering analogy into evidence of misconduct. It helps identify the controls worth checking.

A single point of failure can be informational. One person may understand why a commitment was made, hold the relationship with a decision-maker and decide which concerns need escalation. Distributing job titles will not necessarily distribute that knowledge. Resilience requires other people to have enough context and authority to make a different decision.

Fault isolation asks where a failure can travel. If hardware demand, customer financing, collateral value and capacity guarantees depend on the same growth assumption, a problem can cross contractual boundaries. A company can meet its individual legal obligations and still participate in a fragile system. The financial map is useful precisely because it makes those common assumptions inspectable.

Observability has a governance counterpart too. A dashboard that reports an approved transaction tells us little about an unreported conflict or a warning that never reached the board. The questions are who can raise a concern, where it is recorded, who must respond and whether the response can be reviewed independently. Clear responsibilities make disagreement actionable.

Succession is the organisation's recovery mechanism. A credible successor needs more than a title: decision rights, access to information, people able to challenge commitments and a process for separating contractual obligations from a predecessor's personal ambitions. The test is whether the institution can continue making sound decisions while its most influential person is absent.

The public record establishes a leadership transition, earlier regulatory scrutiny, contested allegations and substantial pre-existing economic dependencies. It leaves the intelligence findings, transaction-level compliance and important questions about oversight unresolved. That is enough to justify serious scrutiny without manufacturing a single hidden cause. [PG01][PG02][CS01][CS03][EM02][EM05]

A successful organisation should make good judgement easier to share and bad decisions easier to challenge. If its resilience depends on one person always being right, the design needs work.
